What the first session locked.

About fifteen people from working groups, Council, and the wider constituency met to discuss the proposed YOUNGO Hub. This is a recap of what the room reached — recommendations, not a ratified policy. Session 2 is Tuesday 8 September at 08:00 UTC.

What the Hub is for

The Hub was presented as an informational place for the constituency, not a replacement for WhatsApp, mailing lists, or Drive. It should be one map: meetings, working groups, submissions, opportunities, and the right contact. The core problem named in the room is that new members cannot find where anything is, and joining now takes about one to one and a half months.

It is meant to work on a low-bandwidth connection. It is still a demo. Council adopts it — or does not — through YOUNGO’s established decision-making process.

Language the first session locked

The room preferred complete ownership over “maximum ownership”, so nobody hears a scale. The draft first article of the data-management discussion is:

  1. YOUNGO has complete ownership and control of the platform.
  2. Individuals hold access only in their YOUNGO capacity, not personally.
  3. Council can revoke access from any individual.
  4. Focal points have access as a minimum safeguard against a hostile takeover.
  5. Developers built it pro bono and expect nothing in return. This must be written and signed.
  6. The Hub is shared YOUNGO infrastructure, not a parallel organisation.

Procedures that keep that true

Ownership language is not enough if credentials stay in personal inboxes. The room named the measures Council’s process should arrange and enforce:

  • Put platform credentials on a YOUNGO Hub email, not personal accounts. Focal points hold that email.
  • Use a dual-key lock: more than one person must authorise server access.
  • Define the operational working-group mandate before forming the team — backend, features, and data management, separate from what members see.
  • Invite an external technical audit before full adoption.
  • A written alternative proposal on ownership is welcome through the usual red-flag path.

Features the room discussed

  • Role-based access for members, contact points, working-group leads, organisations, and admins.
  • Working groups can keep their own section, or simply point to their WhatsApp.
  • No public chat. Members can write to mandate holders. Mandate holders do not start that contact. Broadcasts stay broadcasts.
  • A search bar that never includes personal contact or guardian fields.
  • A calendar per working group, plus opportunities that are not YOUNGO endorsements.
  • Membership Team keeps the human review: approve or decline on the same criteria — under 35, a real person.

Data and security, as presented

  • Individual accounts. Passwords and sessions stored only as hashes.
  • Access granted by role and assignment, not by possessing a file link.
  • The existing YOUNGO privacy policy and code of conduct must be accepted. The version is recorded.
  • Who accessed what, and when, is written to an audit log.
  • The Hub is currently hosted as a demo. Moving credentials off personal accounts is part of making ownership real.

What is still open

  • The consultation so far has reached working groups, Council, and GCT more than the wider constituency. The mailing list is currently broken, which limits mass notice.
  • Who legally holds the domain, the server, and the codebase still has to be decided by Council.
  • The scope of an external technical audit is not yet set.
  • Session 2 should confirm or amend Monday’s language, then assign owners to the procedures.
