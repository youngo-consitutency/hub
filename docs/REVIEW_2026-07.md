# Hub review — July 2026

Code and functionality review of YOUNGO Hub, plus a proposal for the road to
COP31 and on-site coordination in Antalya.

Reviewed at commit `68fc69a`, on a 375×812 mobile viewport and on desktop,
signed in as a member and as an admin holding every role.

---

## 1. What was wrong, and what is now fixed

### 1.1 Anonymous callers could read meeting join links (security)

`eventView()` deleted the `meetingUrl` field, but the same Google Meet links
were repeated in event `description` prose and in working-group `resources[]`,
neither of which was redacted. Both are served to unauthenticated callers by
`/api/events`, `/api/groups`, and `/ics/all.ics`.

Anyone could fetch the public calendar and walk into an ACE or Finance WG call:

```
BEGIN:VEVENT
SUMMARY:ACE WG — post-SB64 / annual summary huddle
DESCRIPTION:... Latest shared Meet link ...: https://meet.google.com/tjp-fcrx-vjt
```

This was a regression from the fixture-seeding commits, and the existing test
`tests/public-routes.test.js` had been failing because of it.

**Fixed** in `server/lib/publicViews.js`: join links are matched by shape
(`meet.google.com/<code>`, `zoom.us/j/`, Teams `meetup-join`, Whereby, Jitsi)
and replaced with `(members-only link)` in public prose; resources that _are_
join links are dropped from the anonymous view. Registration pages
(`zoom.us/meeting/register`, Teams event pages) survive — those are published on
purpose. Verified live: anonymous `/api/groups` returns 56 resources with zero
join links; a verified member gets 57 including the Meet room.

### 1.2 Published announcements never reached Home (correctness)

`assembleFeed()` took `announcements.filter(pinned).slice(0, 2)` with no sort
and no expiry check. Consequences, all live in production data:

- Three fixture announcements were pinned; the third could never appear.
- A pin published through the content workflow never appeared either — the two
  fixture pins held both slots permanently. `tests/content-workflow.test.js`
  had been failing on exactly this.
- On 28 July, Home's most prominent slot showed two calls to action that had
  closed on 27 July, both rendering a "closed" countdown chip.

**Fixed** in `server/lib/feed.js`: pins whose `ctaDeadlineAt` has passed drop
out, and the rest rank by deadline then by publication date. Home now shows the
GYS call closing 31 July instead of two dead ones.

### 1.3 Mobile users could not sign out (functionality)

Sign out existed only in `.sidebarFooter`, and `.sidebar` is `display: none`
below 900px. The Profile page had no sign-out control, and the "More" sheet
listed navigation entries only. On a phone there was no way to end a session —
on a constituency app where members share devices.

**Fixed**: sign out and search now live in the mobile sheet, and the "More" tab
is shown for any signed-in account rather than only verified ones, so a member
still working through the membership course can sign out too.

### 1.4 Touch targets (mobile)

24 of 42 interactive targets on the mobile Home page were under 44×44 CSS px:

| Control                                 | Was  | Now           |
| --------------------------------------- | ---- | ------------- |
| `.btn-sm` — "Apply", "Join", "WhatsApp" | 32px | 44px          |
| Section links — "Calendar →", "All →"   | 14px | 44px          |
| `.sheet .navItem`                       | 39px | 48px          |
| Floating theme toggle                   | 42px | 44px          |
| Feedback trigger                        | 39px | 44px          |
| Inline links in card titles             | 20px | 44px hit area |

**Fixed** with one new sheet, `src/styles/parts/touch.css`, gated on
`(pointer: coarse), (max-width: 900px)` so desktop density is untouched. Inline
links inside sentences cannot take block padding without breaking the line box,
so they get a transparent `::after` overlay instead — verified by hit-testing
±16px from the text centre. Form inputs also get `font-size: max(16px, 1em)`,
which is what stops iOS Safari zooming the page when a field takes focus.

Result on mobile Home: 24 undersized targets → 0.

### 1.5 Navigation had too many sections

The sidebar built a new section per role. An admin saw **7 headings over 21
destinations**, and `/staff/points` appeared **twice** — the de-duplication only
looked inside the "Your workspaces" section, so React logged
`Encountered two children with the same key: /staff/points` on every render.

The mobile "More" sheet was worse: a flat, unlabelled list of 16 entries in a
210px-wide corner popover that ran from y=85 to y=732 with no way to scroll it
and no way to dismiss it by tapping away.

**Fixed**: navigation is now declared as one flat list and grouped afterwards,
with global de-duplication by href. Four headings, each answering one question:

| Section               | Answers                    | Contents                                           |
| --------------------- | -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| What's on             | What is happening?         | Home, Calendar, Opportunities                      |
| Take part             | What do I act on?          | Submissions, Youth Statement, Council, COY tracker |
| People & groups       | Who is there?              | Working groups, Directory, NGO recognition         |
| Your responsibilities | What am I accountable for? | every role-gated tool                              |

Every role-gated destination lands in the last section instead of earning a
heading of its own. An ordinary member sees **3 sections / 10 items**; an admin
holding every role sees 4 and 21. The mobile sheet now carries the same
headings, is a full-width bottom sheet with a backdrop and its own scroll, and
holds search and sign out.

### 1.6 Notifications were half-built

The server side was complete and unused. `server/routes/push.js`,
`server/lib/pushStore.js`, the `push` and `notificationclick` handlers in
`public/sw.js`, migrations 016/017, and `npm run setup:push` all existed — and
**nothing in the client ever called any of it**. Every push helper in
`src/lib/pwa.js` was dead code. There was no way for a member to turn
notifications on, and no instructions anywhere.

**Shipped**: a per-device Notifications section on Profile
(`src/components/NotificationSettings.jsx`) that

- reports whether the browser supports push, whether this deployment has VAPID
  keys, and whether this device is subscribed;
- shows exactly one state at a time — telling a member to unblock notifications
  in their phone settings when the _server_ has no keys sends them to fix
  something that was never broken;
- turns notifications on and off, and sends a test to their own device;
- **detects iPhone and iPad in a Safari tab and shows the Add to Home Screen
  steps in place of a button that cannot work** — iOS only delivers Web Push to
  a home-screen install on 16.4+;
- gives a recovery path once permission has been denied (iOS: Settings ›
  YOUNGO Hub › Notifications), which is otherwise a dead end because the
  browser will not prompt again;
- carries a collapsed reference for iPhone, Android, and desktop, including
  that Focus modes hold alerts back and that each device is separate.

Two supporting changes:

- `POST /api/push/test` no longer requires admin. It only ever reaches the
  caller's own subscriptions, and confirming that alerts actually arrive is the
  point of the setting.
- Its rate limit is now keyed on the session rather than the caller IP. At a COP
  the whole delegation shares one venue network, so an IP-keyed budget would let
  the first ten people setting up notifications lock out everyone else.

`src/lib/pwa.js` also no longer awaits `navigator.serviceWorker.ready`, which
never settles when no worker is installed — the normal state in local
development, where the worker is deliberately unregistered. It would have hung
the settings card indefinitely.

### 1.7 Smaller fixes

- `manifest.json` app shortcuts pointed at `/messages`, a route removed in
  migration 019 — the shortcut opened the 404 page. Replaced with Calendar,
  Opportunities, and Search.
- The group card rendered the monogram and the group name as two adjacent links
  to the same URL: a duplicate tab stop and an easy mis-tap. The monogram is now
  decorative.
- `.claude/launch.json` pointed the `youngo-hub` dev server at `youngo-hub/`,
  which holds no `package.json`. Corrected to `youngo-hub/YOUNGO HUB`.

**Verification**: `npm run check` passes — content check, format, lint, 169
tests, build. Two tests that were failing before this work now pass, and the
suite grew by eight covering the leak, the pin ordering, the iOS gate, and the
push routes.

## 2. Findings not yet addressed

Ranked by what they cost the constituency.

**1. The membership policy gate blocks returning sign-in.** Every visitor must
open the full policy and scroll to the end before reaching the sign-in form —
including an existing member signing in on a new phone. The acknowledgement is
stored in `localStorage`, so it re-triggers on every new device, every browser,
and after any storage clear. Suggested: gate registration on the policy read,
not sign-in; store the acknowledgement on the account so it follows the member.
This is the first thing anyone at COP31 will hit on a borrowed laptop.

**2. Public API responses carry `Cache-Control: public, max-age=60` with
`Vary: Origin` only.** The verified-member variant of the same URL correctly
sets `no-store`, so a private response cannot be stored — but a member can be
served a cached anonymous (redacted) response from a shared cache inside the
60-second window and silently lose join links. Adding `Vary: Cookie` to the
public routes closes it.

**3. The registration form is one very long page.** On a 375px viewport it runs
to roughly a dozen screens: entity type, age band, personal details, password,
background, region, working groups, four policy agreements, consent. Expect
drop-off. Suggested: split into the three steps the form already implies, with
progress saved between them.

**4. No notification triggers exist.** The delivery layer is now reachable, but
nothing calls `/api/push/send` except a human. See §3.

**5. `POST /api/push/send` has no UI.** Admin broadcast is a route with no way
to reach it from the app. See §3.

**6. Page-level consolidation is still possible.** Section count is fixed;
destination count is not. Three surfaces answer NGO questions (`/ngo`,
`/recognition`, `/staff/points`) and two answer process questions
(`/submissions`, `/council`). Merging them is a product decision, so it is
proposed rather than done — see §4.

## 3. Notifications: what to send

The delivery path is live. What it needs now is domain events wired to it. In
rough order of value:

| Trigger                                                        | Source already in the codebase       |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Submission or objection window closing in 72h / 24h            | `feed.js` already computes `closing` |
| A call you are in starts in 15 minutes                         | `feed.js` `live` / `week`            |
| An announcement is pinned                                      | content workflow publish step        |
| An opportunity matching your WG opens                          | `opportunities.js`, `wgInterests`    |
| Your NGO seat invitation, affiliation, or points award changes | `invitations.js`, `points.js`        |
| Your feedback ticket is answered                               | `feedback.js`                        |

Two things to build alongside them:

- **Per-category preferences and quiet hours.** A COP day runs 08:00–23:00 local
  and the constituency is in every time zone. Without categories the first noisy
  week trains everyone to turn notifications off, and they will not come back.
- **An admin broadcast composer** for `/api/push/send`: audience (all / a working
  group / a team), a preview, and the audit entry the route already writes. This
  is the on-site rapid-response channel; it should not be a curl command.

## 4. Proposal — road to COP31 and on-site in Antalya

COP31 is 103 days out. Split into what earns its keep before travel and what
only matters in the venue.

### 4.1 Before COP31 (August – October)

**P0 — Notification triggers and preferences** (§3). Everything below assumes
members actually receive alerts.

**P0 — Fix the sign-in gate** (§2.1). Onboarding volume peaks right before a
COP; this is the funnel.

**P1 — Delegation and badge tracker.** Who holds a YOUNGO badge for which week,
with the swap and reallocation trail. Badge allocation is one of the constituency's
sharpest recurring pains and currently lives in spreadsheets. The `ngo_seats` and
capability model already fits the shape.

**P1 — Travel readiness checklist.** Visa route for Türkiye, insurance, arrival
logistics, accessibility needs, safeguarding briefing — per-member progress,
content-backed like the membership course. Reduces the same questions in every
WG channel.

**P1 — GYS delivery countdown.** The Global Youth Statement has a hard delivery
date and a consultation chain across working groups. A single view of which WG
inputs are in, in review, or missing, with escalation to contact points, turns
the last three weeks from chasing into tracking.

**P2 — First-timer track.** A COP-specific extension to onboarding: how badges,
plenaries, and constituency coordination actually work. The Library and course
machinery already supports it.

### 4.2 During COP31 (Antalya, November)

**P0 — Rapid-response broadcast.** Plenary moved, intervention slot granted,
action at 14:00, room changed. The admin composer from §3, targeted to a working
group or to everyone, audited. Nothing else in the Hub can do this.

**P0 — Offline venue pack.** UNFCCC venues have unreliable connectivity and the
service worker currently refuses to cache any `/api/` response — correct for
account data, wrong for the day's schedule. Add an explicit, member-consented
venue cache: today's sessions, room locations, WG meeting points, and the
on-call contact list, readable with no signal. Everything else stays `no-store`.

**P1 — Today view, in venue time.** A single "now and next" screen keyed to
Antalya local time with room and location. Events currently have no location
field — that is the schema change this needs.

**P1 — Speaking slots and interventions tracker.** Who speaks, on which agenda
item, when, and whether the text is drafted, reviewed, or delivered. The
submissions draft → review → publish workflow already models this; it needs a
COP-shaped view.

**P1 — One-tap safeguarding contact.** A persistent way to reach the
safeguarding focal point from any screen. The Code of Conduct and safeguarding
policies are already binding on members; the app should make acting on them a
single tap for a young person alone in a venue.

**P2 — Bilateral and Party engagement log.** Who met which delegation, what was
asked, what follow-up is owed — feeding the daily debrief.

**P2 — Daily debrief capture into Hub Intelligence.** End-of-day notes per WG,
retaining citations, under the existing governance invariants in `AGENTS.md`.

### 4.3 Information architecture

Section count is fixed. If destination count should drop further, the honest
merges are:

- **One NGO surface.** `/ngo` (portal), `/recognition` (public board), and
  `/staff/points` (awarding) are one subject seen from three angles. As one page
  with tabs, they would occupy one nav slot instead of three.
- **One "process" surface.** `/submissions` and `/council` are both "a thing with
  a deadline you can input on". A combined view filtered by kind would drop
  another slot and put every deadline in one place — which is what a member
  actually wants during a COP.

Both are product decisions, not cleanups, so neither is done here.

## 5. Notes for whoever picks this up

The working tree had unrelated in-flight work when this review started —
`src/components/auth/`, `server/lib/jsonFile.js`, and edits across
`AuthGate.jsx`, `FormControls.jsx`, and several `server/lib` files. Those are
not part of this review. Running `npm run format` also reformatted files that
were already unformatted on `main` (`scripts/research/*`, `GysPolicyTeam.jsx`,
`Statement.jsx`, `store.js`, `gysImport.js`, `cards.css`). Separate the commits
accordingly.

Deployment: notifications need `VAPID_PUBLIC_KEY`, `VAPID_PRIVATE_KEY`, and
`VAPID_SUBJECT` on the Railway service. Generate with `npm run setup:push`.
Until they are set the settings card correctly reports that the deployment has
notifications switched off. Local passing tests are not a release — take it to
staging and smoke-test with an authenticated account first.
